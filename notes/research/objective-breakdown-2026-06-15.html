Section 3 — What the Breakdown Shows

Insight Summary

z1 is largely conquered on set1 (z1=0 on prob_1/2/3/4/7/8/10/16/18 — roughly half). For these instances wins and near-misses are decided by z2 (load balance) + z3 (preference) micro-differences where our packing produces better load distribution (prob_1 −78.9%, prob_8 −12.8%) or nearly ties (prob_2 −0.1%).

The remaining large set1 objectives (prob_11/12/13/14/20) are residual-z1 × huge w1 on congested bays. Even small z1 (e.g. prob_11 z1=24, w1=22857) generates 548K in c1 alone — 96% of that instance's total. prob_12/13/14/20 suffer a double blow: both z1>0 (crane blocking) and z3 has exploded 10–30× above competitor, suggesting bay assignments are systematically wrong for preference on the hardest multi-layer instances.

On set2, the objective is almost universally z1-dominated. Only prob_29 (z1=0, z3-dominant) and prob_29 represent cases where crane scheduling was achieved but preference deviation remains large. The single biggest contributor is prob_38 (250 blocks / 3 bays, w1=13333, z1=6161 → c1=82M, 97% of set2 leader). prob_27 (3,858 blocks, 51M) and prob_39 (31M) are next. Solving set2 z1 is the single highest-leverage action for total objective reduction.

z2 (load imbalance) is never the primary driver — it contributes at most ~20% (prob_1, prob_3) and is usually <5%. The competitive gap on set1 is structurally about z1 persistence on large instances and z3 preference assignment quality on the ≥200-block problems.
